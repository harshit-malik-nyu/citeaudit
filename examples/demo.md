## 1. Background

Machine learning methods have been applied to administrative decision systems
with increasing frequency over the past decade. The foundational review of deep
learning methods remains widely cited [1], and the transformer architecture [2]
underpins most contemporary natural language systems used in document
processing pipelines.

Attention-based models were subsequently adapted for bidirectional language
representation [3], which enabled the classification approaches now common in
automated correspondence triage.

## 2. Compliance frameworks

Several jurisdictions have deployed automated compliance assessment in welfare
administration. The Australian experience has been examined in detail [4], and
comparative work covering European implementations followed [5].

Legal scholarship has questioned whether such systems satisfy administrative
law requirements for reasoned decision-making [6].

## 3. Evaluation

Standard benchmarks for classification performance are well established [7].
Recent work on calibration under distribution shift is also relevant [8].
